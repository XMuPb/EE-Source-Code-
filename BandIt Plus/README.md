# BandIt Plus

Bannerlord treats every bandit party as a loot piñata with legs. I got tired of that, so I fixed it. In BandIt Plus you can rein in beside a war-band of Frost Reavers and talk instead of drawing steel. Ask about their gods. Trade with them. Buy their peace, if your reputation and your purse can carry it. Walk into their hideout as a guest, drink at the fire, run errands for the chief until he trusts you with the one job he wouldn't trust to anyone else. The outlaws here have names and long memories: Granny Hod wants his wood-village's grain back; Centurion Aurelius, the eagle of his disgraced legion. And in a remote camp somewhere a warlord called Vaykos is quietly counting the years until he declares war on the entire map at once. When you choose violence anyway and creep into a hideout by night, there's an alarm system waiting to catch you. Live as a bandit, or hunt them properly for once. The mod doesn't care which.

## Getting started as a bandit

At campaign start a book-styled screen, "Prologue: The Choosing," offers three permanent origins. Outlaw Blood is the easy road: Roguery and Scouting in the blood, bandit clans count you kin, and their peace costs half. Drifter is the middle road, with broad skills, a fuller purse, parley at par, and no past. Lawkeeper is the hard one: trained in sword and command, honour and valour traits, +25% bounties, and every bandit in Calradia hunts you. Each origin grants a balanced kit of skill XP, focus points, attributes, traits, gold, a starting item, and starting chief relations.

The mod adds 8 custom bandit clans alongside vanilla, not replacing it. Frost Reavers (northern heavy 2H-axe infantry), Marsh Stalkers (wetland javelin and bow skirmishers), Highwaymen (roadside sword-and-crossbow ambushers), Slaver Caravans (desert saber-and-lance cavalry), Fallen Legionaries (Empire deserters in a pilum shield-wall), Sky Raiders (mountain pure-bow archers), Steppe Wolves (horse archers), and the Pagan Cult of the Old Gods (forest cultists). 67 new troops total, each clan with its own culture, party template, dark-palette banner flown consistently from map nameplate to scoreboard, and a unique sellable keepsake. They spawn by biome-appropriate substitution. Result: ~3 in 10 bandit encounters become a BandIt Plus clan (MCM-tunable), with per-clan toggles and clean mid-save removal.

Peaceful encounters are the heart of it. Meet any bandit party and you get a full parley tree: talk, lore, history, hideout directions, lord gossip, bribe, trade, threaten, religion, pleasantries, or just fight. All 14 bandit cultures (8 custom plus 6 vanilla) have unique authored responses. Bribing a chief for peace is a relation-modulated 50/50 roll; a refusal returns your gold but costs -3 relation and a 3-day cooldown. Peace is earned per clan, parley by parley. No shortcuts.

Your reputation has a name, too. Destroying war-bands and earning peace both climb an Infamy ladder of six ranks: Footpad, Reaver, Brigand-Captain, Outlaw Lord, Bandit Warlord, and finally Scourge of Calradia. Rank-ups arrive as toasts, and a map crest can show your current rank on screen if you turn it on.

## Chiefs, trust and camps

Every one of the 14 cultures has a named chief with an authored voice, from Vargolf the Iron-eyed to Hakkun the Sand-Reader, plus a named fence contact like Magda the Fence or Salt-Wife Ingrun. Trust runs per culture in tiers 0 through 3, shared across all hideouts of that culture. Chiefs refuse strangers until you've walked into a camp once. Tier 1 unlocks Trade, Doctor and Map. Tier 2 unlocks the Armory, Recruit, and culture-specific services. Tier 3 brings high-end errands that want smithing materials.

Camps are walkable. Step into a hideout in 3D and about 25 NPCs are living in it: the boss with two bodyguards, posted guards, sparring pairs, vendors, people sitting in chairs doing nothing in particular. A greeter meets you on your first visit, and every camp NPC has ambient dialog if you stop to listen. Hold Alt for nameplates over the boss and vendors; the chief shows a gold "!" when he has work for you. Press F near him and he speaks under his own name. Two vendors (food and gear) open a real trade UI with 6-10 randomized items per visit, drawn from pools of 13 food and 24 gear items. The camp menu itself carries Talk, Trade, Armory, Doctor, Recruit, Drink, Eavesdrop, Rest and Map, plus 4 unique culture-flavored services per clan: 56 in all, with time-of-day flavor text. Chiefs also get real encyclopedia pages with portraits and working search, their portraits sit in the quest-giver slot of trust-quest journal entries, and hideout map tooltips finally show an owner.

## The quests

Eight quest types ship: trust errands, vendor quests, named-target hunts, slaver quests, an alliance chain, raid-village quests, hunt-pledges, and the signature quests. The alliance chain and hunt-pledges are longer-running arrangements with a clan; the chiefs explain their terms better than I can here, and the journal keeps track. The trust ladder alone is ~110 authored errand variants across 14 cultures and 3 tiers, with a daily-rotating offer, so the same chief wants different things tomorrow.

The signatures are the ones I'm proud of. Every chief has exactly one bespoke quest, offered in his own voice, once per campaign, unlocked at trust tier 2 with his culture while you're at peace with his clan (and behind an EnableSignatureQuests toggle if you'd rather skip them entirely):

- **Vargolf the Iron-eyed, "The Iron Price"** — seize a lord's iron convoy before winter; the Reavers need axe-heads more than his garrison needs steel.
- **Skarvac of the High Pass, "The Mountain's Toll"** — a rich column ran the pass waving a lord's paper; collect 2400 denars by strength, settle for half, or take it in blood.
- **Granny Hod, "The Widows' Grain"** — tax-riders emptied the wood-village that fills the camp's pot; no bargains.
- **Mireborn, "The Drowned Road"** — a royal surveyor is staking a causeway through the marsh; break the column and the charts go to the black water.
- **The Reeve, "The Reeve's Arrears"** — a merchant combine ran his roads unpaid for a season; collect principal plus interest, compounded quarterly by insult.
- **Khan Borchu, "Forty Hooves"** — stampede forty steppe horses by night or ride them down in the open; two of the best are yours after.
- **Khalid the Chain, "Break the Rival's Chain"** — a rival ring's captain offers 3000 denars to look away; take it and you've betrayed Khalid forever.
- **Centurion Aurelius, "The Lost Standard"** — an imperial column carries the eagle of his disgraced Twelfth; demand it, buy it quietly, or face a legion's drill line.
- **Ulan Twice-Banished, "The Third Writ"** — a khan's emissary carries the third banishment-and-bounty writ ever written against Ulan; he wants to own the paper that hunts him.
- **Aerlin Cliffborn, "The Caged Cliffs"** — nine cliff-falcons netted from the eyries ride south in wicker; the birds come home to the wind.
- **Crone Vael, "The Barrow Spoils"** — grave-diggers sold the old dead to a collector; come by dark, as the dead come.
- **Boll the Eldest, "The Ditch-Folk's Coin"** — the lord's levy squeezed the families that feed the looters; make the counting stop.
- **Bjornulf Salt-Tongue, "The Shore-Price"** — a harbormaster auctioned a beached crew's shares; nine piles on sail-cloth, weighed the old way.
- **Hakkun the Sand-Reader, "The Well-Killers"** — quicklime wagons roll for the deep desert wells; water is life.

Accepting one opens a journal entry that leads with the chief's full speech. Roadside heralds point at waiting jobs. Targets spawn as real parties out on the roads near the chief's camp with a blue tracker. Confrontations open in conversation with real choices: strength demands, renown demands, buy-outs, half-settles, bribes, or steel, and a failed demand stays spent. Every speaker carries an authored personal name (Master Cavren, Guild Factor Hamon, Engineer Fazil), not "Looter." Rewards are gold, chief relation, and per-quest extras like horses or food-vendor trust. Betraying a chief permanently costs a trust tier and his respect.

## Hideout raids

Assaulting a hideout is now an actual fight. Defenders gain extra troops (up to +50%) and tier upgrades scaling with campaign age and an MCM boss-strength setting (0.5-2.5), they get the smart battle AI, and unlike field bandits they fight to the death. A toggleable alarm system keeps you honest. Before the fight, a crimson "HIDEOUT ASSAULT" card shows the hideout name, the chief with banner crest and a taunt, defender stats, a 5-pip difficulty meter, and a spoils preview, with the gold battle-AI panel alongside it.

Sneak in instead and the Shadow Bar takes over: a bottom-center stealth HUD that's invisible while you're safe. Amber suspicion builds while a bandit is close to noticing you. Get spotted and the engine's hidden fail timer becomes a red draining bar; break line of sight or silence the witness and it resets with a green CLEAR flash. A WATCHERS counter tells you how many eyes are on you, and the chief card restyles to "HIDEOUT INFILTRATION" with a live RISK readout. The vanilla game shows you none of this. It was always tracking it.

## Bandits that fight back

Bandit Power makes bandit and looter parties grow with the campaign (default ramp 6 years, party sizes tunable up to 500) through passive growth, village conscription, hideout reinforcement, and absorbed victories. They upgrade along their troop trees into combined-arms compositions and get bolder on the map. In battle they use real tactics now: infantry advance, archers skirmish, cavalry flanks, horse archers harass. Outnumbered roughly 3:1 they cut their losses and withdraw. They also deal bonus damage to your mount (default 2x, configurable 1-4x) because cavalry kiting was embarrassing for everyone. A gold "BANDIT AI" panel appears for ~15 seconds showing power tier (EARLY through LEGENDARY), composition, and aggression. Winning pays: Spoils of Victory awards scaling coin, a culture keepsake, and themed gear via a non-pausing popup. And bandits act without you: hideouts muster raid parties against nearby villages, escalating year over year and gated by per-settlement cooldowns, and strong warbands blockade towns and castles, draining prosperity, security, food and loyalty hourly until you break them in a field battle (which pays gold, renown, and faction relation for your trouble) or the blockade is left to run and ends in a one-time sack.

## The Bandit King arc

Ignore a blockade long enough and the town breaks: loyalty collapses and the settlement rebels as an independent rebel clan wearing the blockaders' identity, culture, banner, and a name built from theirs plus "Uprising," with the warband itself as garrison. A real bandit-held stronghold that you or the AI lords must besiege to retake. Toggleable, if that's too much for your campaign.

Then there's Vaykos, the Mad King. A hidden warlord clan builds strength in secret at a remote hideout, at peace with everyone, for a configurable number of years (default 5, range 1-10). Then the turn fires, once. He declares war on ALL kingdoms, promotes lieutenant lords with their own parties and fleets, and wages genuine conquest: sieges, captured fiefs, naval-aware, no fief cap, until stopped or ruling Calradia alone. Three Warlord Brain toggles (all on by default) make him fight like a calculating lord: he picks weak targets and lifts sieges against relief armies, develops his fiefs from his real war chest, and runs diplomacy, hunting one prey kingdom at a time while suing for peace with kingdoms too strong.

## Captivity and jailbreak

If bandits capture you, they march you bound to their hideout and jail you in a brigands' pit. Wait it out, attempt escapes, or bribe a guard, with configurable escape difficulty. Or take the door: a full stealth jailbreak drops you into a dungeon cell scene as the prisoner, sneaking or fighting past 5-9 patrolling guards to the exit. Getting spotted starts an alarm timer and the guards stop taking prisoners. Getting caught means a beating, the pit, and a harder next attempt.

## The Royal Codex

Settings live in-game in the Royal Codex, a book-styled console opened from a sword button on the map bar. Chapters, animated open, Escape to close, and changes persist across quit and reload. Its HUD chapter has 11 toggles covering every BP HUD element: map crest/rank preview (default off, it's a preview), rank-up toasts, glow, quest alerts, the hideout chief card, the sneak Shadow Bar, the battle AI panel, the hideout alarm system, the Bandit King panels, rebellion-as-toasts, and bandit-only rebellion scoping. The MCM page itself stays slim: master toggle, compatibility mode, debug options, relations reset. Compatibility mode makes BandIt Plus yield to other bandit-spawning mods.

## Requirements and load order

Bannerlord 1.3.x and 1.4.x, including v1.4.5 War Sails, with the same DLL. The dependency stack is the standard one:

1. Bannerlord.Harmony (v2.4.2.225+)
2. Bannerlord.ButterLib
3. Bannerlord.UIExtenderEx
4. Bannerlord.MCMv5
5. BandIt Plus

Save-safe by design. Disabling a clan mid-campaign auto-cleans its parties on the next load with a notification, and the master toggle removes everything. Adding/removing the mod mid-save is supported but may orphan some parties; they'll despawn over time as the game's bandit lifecycle replaces them.
